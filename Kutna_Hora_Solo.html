Playing Kutná Hora Solo

Choose one, two or three rivals in New game. With three rivals the game lasts five rounds. With one or two it lasts six. Two-player games use event cards. Events are optional on the larger boards.

Take your turn. Play two cards on each of your first two turns and one on your last. Pick the card half and target, then confirm. With multiple rivals, Play rivals continues until your next card, tax decision or final scoring.

Set up your guilds. Choose your set from the printed setup card and arrange your house rows. The seed assigns the remaining sets and the rivals’ row order.

Build your income. Take Rights to a building, claim a Plot, then use Building. A Mine can also start ore production. The Income action collects the current value of your production. The market prices affect all players.

Follow the grid. Town and mine coordinates are read left to right, top to bottom. Colored dots are printed scoring matches. A striped guild offer can sell Rights while new construction of that guild is unavailable.

Win on points. Patricians score in the last three rounds. The final count adds town matches, mine row awards, final income, reputation and unused items to earned points. Coins break a points tie. Any players still tied for first share the win.

Check public information. Open a tile to inspect its costs and effects. Use Details when the tile is an action target. Each player board shows the full house rewards, production and supplies. Each rival's remaining cards are below the turn controls.

Resume or share a position. This browser keeps your game and up to five recent Undo steps when local storage is available. If another tab advances the save, choose Load saved game to continue from that position. Export a JSON save to move between browsers or share a position. Version 0.1.0 saves keep their existing deck order.

Rival settings. Standard and Quick consider the next player's likely card. Planner tries the replies on a copy of the board, explores several possible draws when choices are close, and looks further ahead for builds and final scoring.

Mincmistr. Choose Play one Plan to see the moves, resources and board spaces changed. The review also shows any automatic round-end effects. Expand Why this move? for the checks and tie-breaks, or tap a rule number to read it offline.

Report a playtest. The journal saves your Playtest note with the game. Use Export playtest report to share the game and note. If autosave is paused, Export game and note in the save warning makes the same kind of copy. Open a report with Import save, then use Load position before this step in the journal if an earlier position is included. Undo returns to the imported position.

Playing on the table. Open Draw only for a shared Plan deck. Draw one Plan for each Mincmistr action card. Rules and reference has the full rules and two printable aids. Use Export deck and Import deck to move the current deck and five recent Undo steps between devices.

Mincmistr levels. Journeyman follows the printed priorities. Apprentice simplifies plot, build and mine choices. Master plays both cards on its last turn in the final three rounds, and Grandmaster does so each round. In three-player games, Master can also double its last turn in rounds 3–6.

    Play one action card

    Skip any check you cannot play. Use the leftmost matching action card. Never use the Joker. Keep the Plan through all bonuses. Bonuses use no additional action card.

    1. Flip a Plan. Reshuffle the discards when the deck runs out.
    2. Cash below 20 g. Try Income.
    3. Two or more pelicans. Try St. Barbara. One pelican is enough on tile 6, 7, 8, 9 or 11.
    4. Plan goals. Take the first possible goal from top to bottom.
    5. Fallback. Take Income if possible. Otherwise, take Mine. If neither is possible, discard the leftmost remaining action card with no effect.

    Action card row. Mine / Joker → Rights / St. Barbara → Mine / Plot → Build / Income → Rights / Income → Build / Plot.

    Construct

    Build with a right and a plot, else Plot if rights outnumber plots and it holds fewer than three plots, else Rights if it holds fewer than two rights.

    Buying Rights. Only affordable own-guild tiles with a house left and enough demand qualify. Maximize production × price, then cheapest, higher market row, leftmost. Skip if none qualify.

    Plot. Most matching dots for any held right (0 with no rights), then lowest cost, then arrow. A free Plot costs 0 g. Build. Highest production (public = 0), matching dots, arrow, earliest acquired right. Count matching dots for public tiles and on the board edge.

    Free rights

    House reward. Take an eligible public right. With no eligible public right, take nothing.

    St. Barbara tile 2. Prefer an eligible public right. Otherwise, take an eligible own-guild right with the highest production × price. With no eligible right, take nothing.

    Rank public rights by their printed rewards: VP + 2 × pelicans granted + 2 × reputation granted. Break ties by higher market row, then further left. The three-right limit applies to both rewards.

    Mine, Income and ties

    Mine. Use a pool miner. If empty, pay one permit fee to recruit up to two reserves; skip if unable. Keep the most stars + ore + VP, then most stars, then first drawn. Place for most row VP gained, then cheapest, fewest own mines in row, arrow.

    Income. Collect income. If it can then pay 10 g, seat an available patrician with the biggest edge of at least 1 VP. Edge is its points per token minus the best opponent’s. Ties go green, silver, yellow, red. Pay taxes when able.

    Arrow. ◀ first space in reading order (top to bottom, left to right). ▶ last in that order.

    Difficulty

    Apprentice skips town points for Plot and Build. For mine placement it uses fewest own mines in row, then cost, then arrow. Journeyman uses the priorities above.

    Master plays both remaining cards on its last turn in the final three rounds. Tougher three-player Master does this in rounds 3–6. Grandmaster does this every round.

    Mincmistr mine choice aid

    The Mincmistr

    A card-driven solo opponent for Kutná Hora · Version 1.2.5 · 15 Nov 2026

    Play against one, two or three Mincmistrs, using the normal board and round count for that table size. A Mincmistr starts like a player, pays every cost and either pays its taxes or loses reputation. It obeys the piece limits and scores the same way at the end. It gets no starting points and no hidden information. It makes its choices with a short checklist, a deck of 15 Plan cards and a fixed row of six action cards.

    There are no dice. Flip one Plan per action card. Most card plays take one or two checks.

    Components

    Print pages 5 to 8 of this file at actual size (100%), on Letter or A4, and cut out the cards. Pages 6 and 8 are optional card backs; print each one on the back of the page before it.

    • 15 Plan cards.
    • 3 reference cards: Card play, Goals and Choices.
    • From the game box: one set of player components per Mincmistr, including six action cards per set.

    1. Setup

    1. Set up the normal game for your table size. Choose your guild set first from the matching row of the guild setup card. Shuffle the remaining sets and deal one to each Mincmistr. Give each Mincmistr its normal player components, coins and reputation. Use the normal rules for the starting player and seat order.
    2. Shuffle the Mincmistr's three guilds to set their order, top to bottom, on its player board.
    3. Shuffle the 15 Plan cards. Put them face down beside its board.
    4. Lay its six action cards face up in a row, in the order shown in rule 2.
    5. Choose a difficulty (rule 9). Journeyman is the standard game.

    2. The row

    The Mincmistr's action cards always sit in this order, left to right:

    1 2 3 4 5 6
    Mine / Joker Rights / St. Barbara Mine / Plot Build / Income Rights / Income Build / Plot

    For each card play, it uses the leftmost card still in its row that shows the chosen action. Remove the card from the row as it is played. At the start of each round, all six cards return to the row in the same order.

    The Mincmistr never uses the Joker. It uses its Mine / Joker card only for Mine.

    For an action chosen under rule 4, skip it if no matching card remains or if it would be illegal or unaffordable.

    3. Plan cards

    Each Plan card shows two goals in priority order and an arrow.

    • Construct: Build, else Plot, else Rights (rule 5.1).
    • Mine: take a Mine action (rule 5.2).
    • St. Barbara: contribute a pelican (rule 5.3).
    • The arrow, ◀ or ▶, breaks ties between spaces (rule 7).

    When the deck runs out, shuffle the discards to form a new deck.

    4. When the Mincmistr plays a card

    Each time the Mincmistr must play an action card, follow these steps. Resolve the chosen action and all its bonuses before playing another action card.

    4.0 Flip. Flip the top Plan card face up onto the discard pile. This is the current Plan card.

    4.1 Short of cash. If it has fewer than 20 g, it takes Income (rule 6.6). Stop here.

    4.2 Pelicans. It contributes to St. Barbara if it has 2 or more pelicans, or if it has 1 pelican and the current St. Barbara tile is a prize tile. Stop here.

    The prize tiles are Seat a patrician (tile 6), Public neighbours (7), Free building (8), Dig twice (9) and Complete the cathedral (11).

    4.3 Plan. Try the goals on the current Plan card, top first. It does the first goal that is possible. Stop here.

    4.4 Nothing worked. Take Income if possible. Otherwise, take a Mine action. If neither action is possible, discard the leftmost remaining action card with no effect.

    Skip any step that is not possible. For example, if it has 12 g but has already played both Income cards this round, skip 4.1 and go to 4.2.

    5. Goals

    5.1 Construct

    Do the first of these that is possible:

    1. Build: if it holds at least one building right and at least one plot.
    2. Plot: if it holds more rights than plots and has fewer than 3 plots.
    3. Rights: if it holds fewer than 2 rights.

    Each one still needs a card in the row and enough coins. Rule 6 gives its choices.

    5.2 Mine

    It takes a Mine action using a miner from its pool. If its pool is empty, it pays for one permit and recruits as many available reserve miners as it can, up to 2. With an empty pool, Mine is not possible if it cannot pay or has no reserve miners. Otherwise, it draws mine tiles as normal and places one (rule 6.4).

    5.3 St. Barbara

    If it has a pelican, it contributes to St. Barbara and resolves the current St. Barbara tile as normal. Rule 6.5 covers any choices on that tile.

    6. Choices

    6.1 Rights

    It buys only affordable rights on offer from its own guilds. The guild's house row must have a house left, and the population or ore track must allow the guild's next building. It can buy a right without a plot, a Build card or enough money for construction. It never buys public rights. Apply these extra checks only when choosing rights to buy. Use rule 6.5 for free rights.

    Of those tiles, it takes the one with the highest income value: production × the current price of that commodity. Ties go to the cheaper tile, then the higher market row, then the tile further left.

    If no qualifying tile is available to buy, Rights is not possible. Go to the next check.

    6.2 Plot

    For Plot and Build choices, count the matching dots the proposed building would make at that space, including matches on the board edge. Count a public tile's matches for placement too. Only owned buildings earn town points at final scoring.

    For each legal plot, find the highest matching-dot count among its held rights. If it holds no rights, give every legal plot a count of zero. Choose the highest count, then the lowest cost, then the Plan arrow. Every legal plot costs 0 g for a free Plot.

    6.3 Build

    Among legal, affordable builds, choose the most production (public buildings count as 0), then the most matching dots at the plot, then the Plan arrow, then the earliest acquired right.

    6.4 Mine tiles

    Of the tiles drawn, it keeps the one with the most stars + ore + VP. Ties go to more stars, then the first tile drawn.

    It places that tile where it gains the most mine-row points right now: the award it would hold in that row after placing, minus the award it holds there now. Use the award table printed on the board for this player count. With two players, a row with 0–3 stars in total pays 2 to the leader and 0 to the other player, 4–6 stars pays 4 and 1, and 7 or more pays 6 and 2. Tied players each receive the full award for their place and occupy all tied positions. A player with no stars in a row scores nothing there. A tile with no stars gains nothing anywhere.

    Ties go to the cheapest space, then the row where it has the fewest mines, then the arrow. If it cannot pay for any space, it discards the draw.

    Free tiles from technology use the same placement rule.

    6.5 Bonus steps

    Keep the current Plan and its arrow for bonus choices. Bonus actions and placements use no additional action card or Plan and do not repeat rule 4. Follow the bonus effect's costs and normal component limits.

    • Free building right (house reward or St. Barbara tile 2): rank eligible public tiles by their printed rewards: VP + 2 × pelicans granted + 2 × reputation granted. Take the highest value. If no public tile is eligible, tile 2 takes an eligible right from its own guilds with the highest income value. A house reward takes only public rights. Break either tie by market reading order (higher row, then further left). If no right qualifies, take nothing. The three-right limit still applies.
    • Remove a house: when an effect lets it choose a row, choose the guild row with the most houses already removed that still has a house. Ties go to the topmost eligible row. A normal guild Build removes a house from that building's guild row. It always takes optional house removals.
    • Seat a patrician from the bank: the colour with the biggest edge (rule 6.6), even if that edge is 0 or less. Ties go green, silver, yellow, red.
    • Score a building (St. Barbara): its building with the most town points. Ties go by the arrow.
    • Dig twice (St. Barbara tile 9): it takes both free Mine actions, as the tile says, using miners from its reserve first. Placement is free; place each tile by rule 6.4.
    • Free plot, free building or extra Income: use rules 6.2, 6.3 and 6.6.

    6.6 Income and patricians

    Whenever it takes Income, it seats a patrician if three things are true: one is available, it can pay the 10 g (counting the Income it collects), and that colour's edge is at least 1. If more than one colour qualifies, it picks the biggest edge. Ties go green, silver, yellow, red. Otherwise it takes plain Income.

    Edge = the points one patrician of that colour gives the Mincmistr in a council scoring, minus the points it gives you. With more than two players, compare it with the best of its opponents, other Mincmistrs included.

    Colour Points per patrician
    Green 1 for each public building next to each of its buildings
    Silver 3 VP per complete pair of pelicans it has used
    Yellow 2 VP if it has a mine in every row; 4 VP if it has at least 2 in every row
    Red 3 for each complete column of removed houses

    7. The arrow

    When spaces tie, the arrow on the current Plan card decides:

    • ◀: the earliest space in reading order. Start at the top row and read left to right.
    • ▶: the latest space in reading order. Start at the bottom row and read right to left.

    This applies to town plots, building placement and mine spaces.

    8. Everything else

    • Taxes: it pays if it can. Otherwise it loses reputation.
    • Events: they affect it exactly as they affect a player.
    • Limits: 3 rights, 3 plots, miners, houses and every other limit apply as normal.
    • End of game: it scores exactly like a player. The highest total wins. Use the normal tie-breaker.

    9. Difficulty

    Level Change from the rules above
    Apprentice Skip point calculations for placement. Plot: lowest cost, then arrow. Build: most production, then arrow, then earliest acquired right. Mine: keep the normal tile choice. Place in the row with the fewest own mines, then the cheapest space, then by arrow.
    Journeyman None. Play the rules as written.
    Master On its last turn in each of the final three rounds (rounds 4–6, or rounds 3–5 with four players), it plays both remaining cards instead of one. Each card is a full card play (rule 4).
    Grandmaster As Master, but in every round.

    Master and Grandmaster give it extra actions only. They never add points, coins or discounts.

    Fine-tuning. Give the double last turn to the last N rounds. Choose N from 0 to the number of rounds in the game. Master is N = 3. Grandmaster uses every round (N = 6 with two or three players and N = 5 with four).

    10. Two or three Mincmistrs

    To face more opponents, set up a three- or four-player game using rule 1. Give each Mincmistr its own row of action cards.

    • They can share one Plan deck. Flip a new card for each card play.
    • Each Mincmistr follows these rules on its own.
    • Wherever a rule compares it with "you", it compares with the best of its opponents, you and the other Mincmistrs alike.
    • All of them play at the same level.
    • For a tougher three-player game, double the last turn in rounds 3–6 (rule 9, N = 4).

    11. Example

    It is round 2. The Mincmistr has 26 g and 1 pelican. It holds one building right and no plot. Its Mine / Joker card is already played. You flip a Plan card showing Mine, then Construct, with arrow ▶.

    At 26 g it is not short of cash. It has only 1 pelican, and the current St. Barbara tile is Free plot (tile 4), which is not a prize tile. So it follows the plan. Mine is possible. It has a miner in its pool, so it plays Mine / Plot, the leftmost Mine card left in its row.

    It draws two tiles and keeps the one with 2 stars, because it has more stars + ore + VP. You lead the top row 2 stars to 0, with 2 stars in that row. In the second row, you have 3 stars and it has 1, out of 4 stars in total. The 2-star tile in the top row gives a total of 4 stars, tied at 2 each, so it moves from 0 to the leader's 4 points: a gain of 4. In the second row it would also tie at 3 stars, but the 6-star total keeps the award at 4 and 1, so its share rises from 1 to 4: a gain of 3. It places the tile in the top row, on the cheapest space there. If two spaces tie, it takes the latest in reading order.

    12. Quick reference

    1. Flip a Plan card.
    2. Fewer than 20 g: Income.
    3. 2 or more pelicans, or 1 on a prize tile: St. Barbara.
    4. Otherwise, the first possible goal on the Plan card.
    5. Otherwise take Income if possible, else Mine, else discard the leftmost card with no effect.

    Take the first possible action. Use the leftmost card in the row that shows it, and never the Joker. Keep the same Plan through all bonuses.
